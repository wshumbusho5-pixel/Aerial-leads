LIFELINE HOME BUYERS

Proprietary Seller Scripts
PAS Team (Property Acquisition Specialists)

SCRIPT 1: THE CURIOSITY OPENER

Best for: First contact with distressed property leads

[RING]

THEM: Hello?

YOU: Hey [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] - I hope I caught you at an okay time. I've got kind of an unusual question for you... (pause - let curiosity build)

THEM: Okay...?

YOU: So I was looking through some properties over on [STREET NAME or AREA], and yours came up on my list for a specific reason - and I'm honestly not even sure if I can help you, but I had to at least call and find out.

THEM: What do you mean? / Help with what?

YOU: Well, I work with homeowners who are looking at their options - people who might be thinking about moving on from a property but maybe don't want to deal with the whole... traditional process. You know, the listing, the showings, the repairs, all of that.

(PAUSE - Let them respond)

IF THEY SAY: "I'm not interested"

YOU: Not interested? (curious mirror, not challenging)

THEM: [They'll usually elaborate]

YOU: That's totally fair - it sounds like you're pretty settled where you are. Can I ask you one quick thing though? Just so I know I'm not wasting your time if I ever call back - is it more that you love the property and couldn't see yourself leaving? Or is it more about timing?

IF THEY SAY: "How'd you get my number?"

YOU: That's a fair question. Your property came up in some research I was doing on [AREA] - I work with a local company called Lifeline Home Buyers, and we reach out to homeowners to see if we might be able to help. Most people tell me no, and that's completely fine. I just didn't want to miss someone who might actually be looking for a simpler way out.

IF THEY ENGAGE: "What kind of help?"

YOU: So let me ask you something - and just think about this for a second...

What would it be like for you if you could skip all the traditional stuff? No having to list it, no spending money on repairs, no strangers walking through your home judging everything, no waiting months wondering if it's actually going to close...

(PAUSE - let them imagine)

What would that kind of simplicity mean for your situation right now?

GETTING TO THE APPOINTMENT (VIRTUAL FIRST)

YOU: Here's what I'm thinking - and you can totally say no - would it be crazy if we just hopped on a quick call to chat more about your situation? Just 10-15 minutes, no pressure, no commitment. I just want to learn a bit more and see if we might be able to help.

What works better for you - sometime this week or next? Morning or afternoon?

(Once they agree, get their email to send calendar invite)

YOU: Perfect. What's the best email to send you a calendar invite? That way you'll have all the details and a reminder.

WHY VIRTUAL FIRST?

  • Lower commitment = easier "yes"
  • Qualifies the lead before sending someone in person
  • Builds rapport and trust before the in-person visit
  • In-person visit happens AFTER the virtual call when they're warmed up

SCRIPT 2: THE EMPATHY OPENER

Best for: Pre-foreclosure, tax delinquent, probate, or high-distress situations

[RING]

THEM: Hello?

YOU: Hey [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] with Lifeline Home Buyers. I know this is probably not a call you were expecting, and I want to be upfront with you - I'm calling about the property on [ADDRESS].

(Pause - let them respond)

THEM: Okay...

YOU: I'm not going to pretend I know your whole situation - but your property came up in some research I was doing on properties that might need some attention. And I work with homeowners who are looking for a way to move forward without all the stress of the traditional process.

I'm not even sure I can help you - that really depends on what you're dealing with. But let me ask you...

(USE ONE OF THESE TWO FORMATS):

Format 1: "Would it be crazy to at least explore what your options might be - even if you decide it's not for you?"

Format 2: "I'm guessing you probably haven't thought about doing anything with the property... or have you?"

IF THEY SHARE THEIR SITUATION

YOU: (Use labeling) It sounds like you've been carrying a lot with this... (or: It sounds like the timing has just been really difficult...)

(Let them talk. Mirror their last few words as questions to keep them opening up.)

YOU: So let me ask you this - if you could wave a magic wand and have this whole thing resolved... what would that look like for you? What would need to happen?

IF THEY GET EMOTIONAL

YOU: (Slow down your voice - Late Night FM DJ tone)

I hear you. And honestly, that's exactly why I called - because I've seen so many people in situations like this try to handle everything alone, and it just makes it harder.

You don't have to figure this out by yourself. Let me ask you one thing - if there was a way to get out from under this without the stress, without the judgment, would that be something worth exploring? Even just a conversation?

SCRIPT 3: THE DIRECT APPROACH

Best for: Absentee owners, landlords, inherited properties

[RING]

THEM: Hello?

YOU: Hey [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] with Lifeline Home Buyers. I'll get straight to the point because I know your time is valuable.

I'm looking at the property on [ADDRESS] - I believe you own it. We buy properties directly from owners, no realtors, no fees, quick close.

I don't know if selling is even on your radar, but if it ever was - or if the right number came along - would that be a conversation worth having?

IF THEY SAY: "What are you offering?"

YOU: Fair question. I can't throw out a number without seeing the place - that wouldn't be fair to either of us. But what I can tell you is we pay fair cash prices, we close fast, and we buy properties in any condition.

What would you need to get out of it to make it make sense for you?

IF THEY SAY: "It's not for sale"

YOU: Got it - not for sale right now. Can I ask though - is that a "never" situation, or more of a "not at this price / not at this time" situation? Just so I know whether to follow up down the road or take you off my list completely.

SCRIPT 4: THE FOLLOW-UP CALL

Best for: People you've already spoken to who weren't ready

[RING]

THEM: Hello?

YOU: Hey [FIRST NAME], it's [YOUR NAME] - we talked a few weeks ago about your property on [STREET]. How are you?

THEM: Oh, hi... I'm fine.

YOU: Good to hear. Hey, I wanted to check back in with you. Not to bug you, but because I had a thought...

When we last talked, you mentioned [REFERENCE SOMETHING THEY SAID]. I was wondering if anything had changed on your end?

SCRIPT 5: THE VOICEMAIL

Max 20 seconds - Create curiosity, don't sell

VERSION 1 - CURIOSITY

"Hey [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME]. I was looking into some properties on [STREET/AREA] and yours came up for a specific reason. I had a quick question for you - give me a call back when you get a chance. My number is [NUMBER]. Again, it's [YOUR NAME], [NUMBER]. Talk soon."

VERSION 2 - VALUE HINT

"Hi [FIRST NAME], [YOUR NAME] here with Lifeline Home Buyers. I'm calling about the property on [ADDRESS]. We've been actively buying in your area and I wanted to see if we could help you out. No pressure, no obligation - just wanted to connect. Call me at [NUMBER]. That's [NUMBER]. Thanks."

OBJECTION RESPONSES

"I'm not interested."
Response: "Totally understand. Can I ask - is it because you're happy with the property, or is it more about timing? I just want to make sure I'm not missing someone who could actually use the help."
"I'm not selling."
Response: "Got it. And I'm not asking you to sell - I'm just asking if you'd be open to knowing what your options are. Sometimes just knowing what a property is worth in cash gives people peace of mind. Would that be worth a 5-minute conversation?"
"How much are you offering?"
Response: "I'd love to give you a number, but I'd be doing you a disservice making something up without seeing the place. What I can tell you is we're fair, we're fast, and we don't play games. What would you need to get out of it for a sale to make sense?"
"I've talked to investors before and they're all lowballers."
Response: "I hear that a lot, and honestly, some of them are. Here's the difference with us - we're not looking to steal anything. We're looking for situations where cash and speed have value. If you need retail, we're probably not the right fit. But if you need simple... that's exactly what we do."
"I need to talk to my spouse/family."
Response: "Of course - that makes total sense. Would it help if I talked to both of you together? That way everyone hears the same thing and can ask questions. When would be a good time for all of you?"
"Send me something in writing."
Response: "Absolutely. What's the best email for you? I'll send over some info about who we are and how we work. And just so you know - there's no pressure. It's just information. What's that email?"
"I want to list it with an agent."
Response: "That's a totally valid option. Can I ask - what's making you lean that direction? Is it the price you think you'd get? Because depending on the condition and your timeline, sometimes the math works out differently than people expect. Would you be open to at least seeing what a cash offer looks like before you decide?"

SPECIAL SITUATION: TAX DELINQUENT LEADS

WHY YOU SHOULD NEVER MENTION TAX ISSUES DIRECTLY

Tax delinquency carries shame. People feel embarrassed, judged, like they failed.

If you say: "I noticed you might be behind on property taxes..."

Their reaction is:

  • "How do they know that?" (feels exposed)
  • "They're trying to take advantage of me" (defensive)
  • "I don't want to talk about this" (shuts down)

Instead: Use vague language that signals "I know something's going on" without naming the specific shame.

VAGUE PHRASES THAT WORK FOR TAX DELINQUENT LEADS

  • "Your property came up in some research I was doing on properties that might need some attention..."
  • "I work with homeowners who might be dealing with some challenges on their property..."
  • "Sometimes properties can have some things that need to be addressed, and I wanted to see if that's something you're thinking about..."
  • "I noticed your property might have a few things going on, and I just wanted to reach out..."

IF THEY BRING UP TAX ISSUES THEMSELVES

THEM: Yeah, I'm behind on the taxes actually...

YOU: (Late Night FM DJ voice - calm, no judgment)

I appreciate you sharing that with me. That's actually something we deal with all the time - you're definitely not alone in this.

Can I ask - how far behind are we talking? And is that something that's been weighing on you?

(Let them talk. Use mirroring. Show empathy, not judgment.)

KEY PRINCIPLE

Create a safe space for them to share. If you call out their shame, they shut down. If you create safety, they open up and tell you everything.

Your job is to be the person they can finally talk to without judgment. When they feel that, they'll tell you about the taxes, the repairs they can't afford, the family issues - everything.

SPECIAL SITUATIONS

These scenarios come up regularly. Reference them when needed.

1. GATEKEEPER (LLCs / Companies)

When: You call and reach a company/receptionist instead of the owner

THEM: "[COMPANY NAME], how can I help you?"

YOU: Hey, this is [YOUR NAME] - I had a quick question about the property on [ADDRESS]. Who handles those decisions?

THEM: What's this regarding?

YOU: We've been looking at a few properties in that area and that one came up. Just wanted to have a quick conversation with whoever manages it - is that something you handle, or is there someone else I should talk to?

(Including them as a potential decision-maker is flattering and often gets you further)

2. WRONG PERSON (But They Might Know Owner)

When: Skip trace gave you the wrong number, but the person might know the owner

YOU: Hey, I'm trying to reach [OWNER NAME] about a property on [ADDRESS]. I don't suppose you'd know how I could get in touch with them?

[If they know the owner]

YOU: Oh perfect. Would you mind passing along my info? Just tell them [YOUR NAME] from Lifeline called about the property - they can reach me at [NUMBER].

I really appreciate you helping me out.

Why "I don't suppose you'd know..."? It's a No-Oriented question. Makes it easier for them to help you.

3. ANGRY ANSWER

When: They answer hostile, aggressive, or clearly annoyed

THEM: "WHAT DO YOU WANT?!" / "WHO IS THIS?!" / [Hostile tone]

YOU: (Slow down. Late Night FM DJ voice. Stay completely calm.)

Hey... sounds like I caught you at a rough time. I can call back - would another day work better?

[They usually soften. If they stay hostile:]

YOU: No problem at all. I'll let you go. Take care.

NEVER argue. NEVER explain. NEVER match their energy.

Your calm is your power. If they stay hostile, exit gracefully. Don't burn the bridge - they might call back later when they've calmed down.

4. CLOSING CONFIRMATION (After Setting Appointment)

When: They've agreed to a call - confirm it clearly to reduce no-shows

YOU: Perfect. So just to confirm - we'll give you a call [DAY] around [TIME] to chat more about your situation. I'll send you a calendar invite so you have it.

If anything changes on your end, just give us a call. Otherwise, we'll talk to you [DAY].

Appreciate your time, [NAME].

Why no "Sound good?" Asking for "yes" is harder than just confirming as a statement. State it, don't ask it.

5. SOFT EXIT (Dead Lead)

When: It's clearly a no - exit gracefully without burning the bridge

YOU: I hear you - totally understand.

I'll take you off our list. If anything ever changes down the road, you've got our number.

Take care, [NAME].

Why this matters: Circumstances change. The person who says "never" today might inherit a problem property next year. Leave them with a positive impression of Lifeline.

GOLDEN RULES FOR EVERY CALL

  1. Use their first name - It's the sweetest sound to anyone
  2. Pause after important points - Let them think and respond
  3. Mirror their energy - If they're calm, be calm. If they're energetic, match it slightly
  4. Never argue - Label their objection, don't fight it
  5. One goal per call - First call = curiosity and appointment. That's it.
  6. Smile when you talk - They can hear it in your voice
  7. Slow down - Fast = nervous and salesy. Slow = confident and trustworthy
  8. Write down what they tell you - Use it in follow-ups to show you listened
