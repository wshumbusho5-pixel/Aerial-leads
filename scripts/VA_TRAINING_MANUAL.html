PART 1: THE PSYCHOLOGY YOU MUST UNDERSTAND

Before you learn the scripts, you must understand WHY they work. Memorizing words without understanding the psychology will make you sound robotic. Understanding the psychology will make you sound natural.

PRINCIPLE 1: THE 7-SECOND RULE

What it is: People decide if you're worth listening to within 7 seconds of answering.

What this means for you: Your opening line is EVERYTHING. If you sound like a typical salesperson in the first 7 seconds, they've already decided to hang up.

How to apply it:

  • Sound casual, not corporate
  • Sound like someone they might know
  • Create curiosity immediately
  • Never start with a pitch

PRINCIPLE 2: CURIOSITY IS AN ITCH

What it is: When there's a gap between what someone knows and what they WANT to know, they feel an uncomfortable itch that must be scratched.

What this means for you: Don't give information freely. Create gaps. Make them WANT to ask you questions.

Example:

  • BAD: "I want to buy your house for cash" (no gap)
  • GOOD: "Your property came up for a specific reason..." (what reason?)

PRINCIPLE 3: PATTERN INTERRUPT

What it is: When someone answers an unknown call, their brain runs an automatic script:

  1. Who is this?
  2. What do they want?
  3. How do I hang up?

What this means for you: You must BREAK this pattern before step 3. Say something unexpected.

  • EXPECTED: "Hi, I'm calling about your property..."
  • PATTERN INTERRUPT: "Hey John, I've got kind of an unusual question..."

PRINCIPLE 4: FAMILIARITY WINS

What it is: Human brains trust what feels familiar. Friends don't ask "Is this John?" - they just start talking.

IMPORTANT:

DON'T SAY DO SAY
"Hello, is this John Smith?" "Hey John, this is Mike with Lifeline..."

PRINCIPLE 5: GIVE THEM AN EXIT

What it is: When people feel trapped, they resist. When they feel free to leave, they often stay.

Phrases that work:

  • "I'm not even sure if this applies to you..."
  • "You may not be interested at all, and that's totally fine..."
  • "Most people tell me no, and that's completely okay..."

PRINCIPLE 6: QUESTIONS BEAT STATEMENTS

What it is: Statements trigger skepticism. Questions trigger imagination.

  • STATEMENT: "We buy houses fast for cash" → They think "Yeah, sure"
  • QUESTION: "What would it mean for you if you could close in two weeks?" → They imagine it

PRINCIPLE 7: MIRROR THEIR WORDS

What it is: Repeating the last 1-3 words someone says (as a question) makes them elaborate and feel heard.

THEM: "I'm not really interested right now."
YOU: "Not interested right now?" (curious tone)
THEM: "Well, I mean... we've thought about it, but the timing isn't right..."

Now they're explaining instead of rejecting.

PRINCIPLE 8: LABEL THEIR EMOTIONS

What it is: When you name what someone is feeling, it defuses that feeling.

Use phrases like:

  • "It sounds like you've been dealing with a lot..."
  • "It seems like timing is the biggest challenge..."
  • "It looks like you've had frustrating experiences..."

PRINCIPLE 9: VOICE TONE > WORDS

Research shows 38% of communication is tone, only 7% is words. HOW you say it is 5x more important.

Tone When to Use How It Sounds
Positive/Playful Opening the call Light, warm, casual
Late Night FM DJ Handling objections Slow, calm, deep
Direct/Assertive Important points Neutral, matter-of-fact

Key Rules: Slow down • Smile while talking • Pause after important points

PRINCIPLE 10: ONE GOAL PER CALL

First call goal: Create curiosity → Get appointment. That's it.

What NOT to do: Don't negotiate price, don't get commitment to sell, don't explain your whole process.

PRINCIPLE 11: NO-ORIENTED QUESTIONS

What it is: People find it easier to say "No" than "Yes." "No" feels safe and keeps them in control. Use this to your advantage by framing questions where "No" actually moves things forward.

The Problem:

  • BAD: "Is there anything going on with the property?" → Easy escape: "No, everything's fine." → Conversation over.
  • BAD: "Would you be interested in selling?" → "No" shuts you down.

The Solution - Two Formats:

Format Example Why It Works
"Would it be crazy to..." "Would it be crazy to at least explore what your options are?" "No, it wouldn't be crazy" = They're open to it
"I'm guessing you probably haven't..." "I'm guessing you probably haven't thought about doing anything with the property... or have you?" "No, that's wrong - actually I have" = They open up

More Examples:

  • "You probably don't have any interest in a simpler way to handle the property, right?" → "No" = "Actually, I might..."
  • "Would it be ridiculous if we hopped on a quick call for 10 minutes?" → "No" = It's not ridiculous
  • "I'm guessing this isn't something you've been losing sleep over?" → "No, actually it has been..."

PRINCIPLE 12: NEVER MENTION SHAME DIRECTLY

What it is: Tax delinquency, foreclosure, code violations - these carry shame. If you call it out directly, they shut down.

What NOT to do:

  • "I see you're behind on property taxes..." → They feel exposed and defensive
  • "I noticed you're in pre-foreclosure..." → Shame triggers walls

What TO do - Use vague language:

  • "Your property came up in some research I was doing on properties that might need some attention..."
  • "I work with homeowners who might be dealing with some challenges..."
  • "Sometimes properties can have a few things going on..."

Key: Create a safe space. Let THEM bring up the specifics. When they feel safe, they'll tell you everything.

PART 3: THE SCRIPTS

THE OPENER - CURIOSITY APPROACH

THEM: Hello?

YOU: Hey [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] with Lifeline - I hope I caught you at an okay time.

[PAUSE - wait for response]

THEM: Yeah... / Who is this?

YOU: I've got kind of an unusual question for you...

[PAUSE - let curiosity build]

THEM: Okay...?

YOU: So I was looking through some properties in [AREA/STREET], and yours came up for a specific reason - and honestly, I'm not even sure if I can help you, but I had to at least call and find out.

THEM: What do you mean? / Help with what?

YOU: I work with homeowners who are looking at their options - people who might be thinking about moving on from a property but don't want to deal with the traditional process. You know, the listing, the repairs, the showings, all of that.

[PAUSE - let them respond]

HANDLING: "I'M NOT INTERESTED"

THEM: I'm not interested.

YOU: Totally understand.

[PAUSE]

YOU: Can I ask you one quick thing? Just so I know whether to follow up later or take you off my list completely -

Is it more that you love the property and can't see yourself leaving? Or is it more about timing right now?

HANDLING: "HOW'D YOU GET MY NUMBER?"

YOU: Fair question.

Your property came up in some research I was doing on [AREA] - I work with a local company called Lifeline Home Buyers, and we reach out to homeowners to see if we might be able to help.

Most people tell me no, and that's completely fine. I just didn't want to miss someone who might actually be looking for a simpler way forward.

HANDLING: "WHAT ARE YOU OFFERING?"

YOU: Fair question.

I can't throw out a number without seeing the place - that wouldn't be fair to either of us.

But what I can tell you is we pay fair cash prices, we close fast, and we handle properties in any condition.

Let me ask you this - what would you need to get out of it for a sale to make sense?

HANDLING: "INVESTORS ARE ALL LOWBALLERS"

YOU: I hear that a lot. And honestly, some of them are.

[PAUSE]

Here's the difference with us - we're not looking to steal anything. We're looking for situations where cash and speed have real value.

If you need top retail dollar, we're probably not the right fit. But if you need simple and certain... that's exactly what we do.

SETTING THE APPOINTMENT (VIRTUAL FIRST)

YOU: Here's what I'm thinking - and you can totally say no -

Would it be crazy if we hopped on a quick call to chat more about your situation? Just 10-15 minutes, no pressure, no commitment. I just want to learn a bit more and see if we might be able to help.

What works better for you - sometime this week or next?

THEM: [Picks a timeframe]

YOU: Perfect. Morning or afternoon?

THEM: [Picks a time]

YOU: Great. What's the best email to send you a calendar invite? That way you'll have all the details and a reminder.

THEM: [Gives email]

YOU: Perfect. I'll send that over right now. Talk to you on [DAY]!

Why Virtual First? Lower commitment = easier "yes." It qualifies the lead and builds trust before anyone visits in person.

THE VOICEMAIL (Under 20 Seconds)

"Hey [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME].
I was looking into some properties on [STREET/AREA] and yours came up for a specific reason.
I had a quick question for you - give me a call back when you get a chance.
My number is [NUMBER].
Again, that's [YOUR NAME], [NUMBER].
Talk soon."

PART 4: CALL FLOW CHECKLIST

Before the Call

  • I know their first name
  • I know their property address or area
  • I'm in a quiet place
  • I'm ready to take notes
  • I've taken a breath and I'm calm

During the Call

  • I said "Hey [Name]" not "Is this [Name]?"
  • I created curiosity in the first 10 seconds
  • I paused after creating curiosity
  • I spoke slowly and calmly
  • I smiled while talking
  • I mirrored their words when they spoke
  • I labeled their emotions when appropriate
  • I asked questions instead of making statements
  • I offered exits ("totally fine if not")
  • I focused on ONE goal: getting the appointment

After the Call

  • I wrote down key information they shared
  • I noted any objections for follow-up
  • I scheduled any follow-up needed
  • I logged the call in the system

PART 5: COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

MISTAKE 1: Talking Too Fast

Fast talking = nervousness = untrustworthy

Fix: Consciously slow down. Pause between sentences.

MISTAKE 2: Not Pausing After Questions

Filling silence kills curiosity.

Fix: Ask a question, then WAIT. Count to 3 in your head.

MISTAKE 3: Arguing With Objections

You can't win an argument with someone you want something from.

Fix: Validate first. "That makes sense." Then ask a question.

MISTAKE 4: Pitching Too Early

They haven't decided to listen yet.

Fix: Create curiosity first. Get them asking YOU questions.

MISTAKE 5: Saying "Is this [Name]?"

Signals you're a stranger making a cold call.

Fix: Say "Hey [Name], this is..." like you know them.
