## Inheriting a Property in Ohio? Here's What to Do Next

Inheriting a house can feel overwhelming, especially when you're grieving. Between probate court, property taxes, insurance, and maintenance, the costs start adding up fast. This guide walks you through the process of selling an inherited house in Ohio so you can make an informed decision.

## Step 1: Determine if Probate Is Required

In Ohio, most inherited properties must go through probate before they can be sold. Probate is the legal process of transferring ownership from the deceased to the heirs.

- **If there's a will:** The executor named in the will files it with the probate court in the county where the deceased lived.
- **If there's no will:** The court appoints an administrator to handle the estate.
- **Transfer-on-death deed:** If the property was held with a TOD affidavit, it may transfer outside of probate entirely.

Probate in Ohio typically takes 6 to 12 months, but properties can often be sold during this period with court approval.

## Step 2: Get Clear Title

Before you can sell, title must be clear. Common title issues with inherited properties include:

- **Multiple heirs** who all need to agree to the sale
- **Outstanding liens** or mortgages from the deceased
- **Back property taxes** that accumulated after the owner's passing
- **Judgments** against the estate

A title company will run a search and identify any issues. These are typically resolved at closing from the sale proceeds.

## Step 3: Understand Your Selling Options

You have three main options for selling an inherited house in Ohio:

### List with a Real Estate Agent
- Typically takes 3-6 months
- Requires repairs, staging, and showings
- Agent commission of 5-6% plus closing costs
- Best for move-in ready properties in good markets

### Sell For Sale By Owner (FSBO)
- Save on commission but handle all marketing and showings
- Still need to make repairs to attract buyers
- Legal paperwork and negotiation falls on you

### Sell to a Cash Buyer
- Close in as little as 7-21 days
- No repairs, cleaning, or showings required
- No agent commissions or closing costs
- Best for properties that need work or when you need to sell quickly

## Step 4: Handle the Tax Implications

When you inherit a property in Ohio, you receive what's called a **stepped-up basis**. This means the property's tax basis is adjusted to its fair market value at the date of death, not the original purchase price.

**Example:** If your parent bought the house for $50,000 and it was worth $120,000 when they passed, your basis is $120,000. If you sell for $125,000, you only pay capital gains tax on the $5,000 difference.

Ohio does not have a state inheritance tax (it was repealed in 2013), but federal estate tax may apply for very large estates.

> Always consult a tax professional for advice specific to your situation.

## Step 5: Deal with the Property in the Meantime

While you're going through probate or deciding what to do, the property still needs attention:

- **Keep insurance active** — homeowner's insurance may lapse after the owner's death
- **Pay property taxes** — delinquent taxes accrue penalties and can lead to tax lien sales
- **Secure the property** — vacant properties are targets for vandalism and code violations
- **Maintain utilities** — pipes can freeze in Ohio winters if heat is turned off

These carrying costs add up quickly, which is why many heirs choose to sell as fast as possible.

## The Bottom Line

Selling an inherited house in Ohio doesn't have to be complicated. The key is understanding your options and acting before carrying costs eat into your equity. Whether you list traditionally or sell for cash, the most important thing is to start the process.
